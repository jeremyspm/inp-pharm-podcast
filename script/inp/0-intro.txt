@ say: How this forecast works

Kia ora. This is the I N P final, the forecast. The exam is on Monday the second of November: forty multi-choice questions at one mark each, and fifteen short answers at four marks each. That's sixty marks of short answer against forty of multi-choice, so the short answers are where this exam is won.

Nobody has posted the real questions, and Lisa sets no practice short answers. So every question in this episode is the podcast's own. But none of them is a guess out of thin air. Each one comes from something the lecturers pointed at: the Exam Content page, which lists twenty-nine themes; Lisa's and Janine's revision decks; the revision session on the twenty-seventh of October, where Lisa ran quizzes on the topics the class voted for; and the lecturers' own Canvas quizzes. Every track starts by saying why it's in.

Here's the order. First, Lisa's half of the course: social determinants, equity, prevention, screening, primary health care, the Health Strategy, Te Tiriti, the standards of competence, therapeutic use of self, family-centred care, pain and discharge. Then Janine's half: the nursing process, holistic assessment, consent, health literacy, vital signs, documentation, infection control, evidence-based practice, self-care, long-term conditions, diabetes and rheumatic fever. Then one track of the one-line facts the multi-choice keeps asking, and a rapid-fire track at the end.

Now the most useful thing Lisa gave us: her marking guide. On the twenty-seventh she showed a four-mark practice question on falls. Part one said identify and describe five strategies. Part two said explain how they reduce harm. Her marks went like this. One mark for identifying the five. One mark for describing them. Two marks for the explain part. So the why and the how carry half the marks. A list of words, on its own, gets you one mark out of four.

Her command words. Identify means name it, briefly. Describe means the what: its key features. Explain means the why or the how. Discuss means go further: examples, both sides, then link it back. Compare means name both things, then say how they relate. Bullet points are fine for an identify part, but not for discuss, because you can't link ideas in bullets. And about a hundred and fifty words is a guide, not a count. Her words: don't just write two sentences.

One more habit from her: use the keywords in the question as the starters of your points. If the question says falls risk, your first line says falls risk.
