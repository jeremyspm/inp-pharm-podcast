@ say: Her keys and the classes

Joan's technique first. Read to the end: the real question is often in the last line. Rule out the obviously wrong options. Always and never are usually wrong. For the best response, pick the one that's most ethical, legal and compassionate, the gentlest. You're a student, so tell the R N can be right. And ignore runs of C: answers are random.

Now the classes, both ways. She said most of them start with anti, meaning it stops something.

Q: Anti-emetic?

Prevents or stops nausea and vomiting. Emesis means vomiting. A very common group.

Q: Antihistamine?

Blocks histamine, for allergy.

Q: Bronchodilator?

Opens the airways, like salbutamol.

Q: Diuretic?

Increases urine output.

Q: Analgesic? Antipyretic? Anti-inflammatory?

Relieves pain. Reduces fever. Reduces inflammation.

Q: A contraindication?

A reason not to give a drug to this person, like a known penicillin allergy for amoxicillin. Definition plus example.

Now her formative keys that haven't come up yet.

Q: The benefit of an enteric-coated tablet?

It resists the digestive action of the stomach.

Q: Most drugs and their metabolites are excreted by which organ?

The kidney. She called that one a gift.

Q: How are topical medicines given?

Directly on to a body surface.

Q: Which is given by the oral route: codeine elixir, a corticosteroid ointment, a paracetamol suppository, or an insulin injection?

Codeine elixir. An elixir is a liquid you swallow.

Q: Which approach best supports health literacy when teaching about a new medicine?

Plain language, and confirm understanding with teach-back.

Q: Which entity monitors the quality and safety of medicines in Aotearoa?

Medsafe.

Q: A drug that binds a receptor and causes a response? One that binds and causes none?

An agonist. An antagonist.

Q: What does A D R stand for?

Adverse drug reaction.

Q: For a drug with very high first-pass metabolism, which route would not be prescribed?

Oral.

{recap} The classes both ways: anti-emetic stops nausea and vomiting; antihistamine blocks histamine; bronchodilator opens airways; diuretic increases urine; analgesic relieves pain; antipyretic reduces fever. Enteric coating resists the stomach. Most drugs leave by the kidney. Topical goes on a body surface. Medsafe monitors safety. Always and never are usually wrong.
